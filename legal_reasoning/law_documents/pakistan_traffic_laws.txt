PAKISTAN TRAFFIC LAWS - COMPILED KNOWLEDGE BASE
================================================
Source 1: Motor Vehicles Ordinance (MVO) 1965
Source 2: National Highway & Motorway Police (NHMP) Rules
Source 3: Pakistan Highway Code
================================================

SECTION 1: LANE DISCIPLINE VIOLATIONS
=======================================

Section 1.1 - Solid Line Crossing Prohibition
Under the Motor Vehicles Ordinance 1965, Section 36, no driver shall cross or straddle a solid white line painted on the roadway. A solid white line indicates that overtaking is strictly prohibited. Any vehicle found crossing a solid white line shall be liable to a fine of PKR 1,000 and may have their driving license suspended for a period of 30 days. The solid line is placed in areas where visibility is restricted, such as bends, hills, or busy intersections, making it extremely dangerous to overtake.

Section 1.2 - Illegal Lane Change
According to NHMP Rules, Chapter 4, Rule 14, a driver must signal at least 30 meters before changing lanes on a highway. Sudden or abrupt lane changes without signaling are considered illegal and constitute a lane discipline violation. The penalty for illegal lane change is a fine of PKR 500 for the first offense and PKR 1,500 for subsequent offenses. On motorways, lane discipline is strictly enforced and vehicles must keep to the left lane unless overtaking.

Section 1.3 - Overtaking Violations
Pakistan Highway Code, Rule 19 states that a driver shall not overtake another vehicle at a pedestrian crossing, intersection, railway level crossing, or on a bend or hill crest where visibility is limited. Overtaking on the wrong side (right side in Pakistan) is strictly prohibited. Violation of overtaking rules carries a penalty of PKR 1,000 and three demerit points on the driving license.

Section 1.4 - Lane Marking Compliance
Under MVO 1965, Section 37, all drivers must comply with road markings including lane markings, stop lines, and directional arrows. Failure to comply with lane markings is an offense punishable by a fine of PKR 500. Double yellow lines indicate no overtaking in either direction. Broken white lines permit lane changes when safe to do so.

Section 1.5 - Wrong Way Driving
NHMP Rules, Chapter 5, Rule 21 strictly prohibits driving against the flow of traffic. Wrong-way driving on a motorway or highway is one of the most dangerous traffic violations and carries a fine of PKR 5,000, immediate license suspension, and possible vehicle impoundment. Wrong-way driving is defined as operating a vehicle in a direction opposite to the designated traffic flow on a one-way road or divided highway.

Section 1.6 - Straddling Lane Markings
According to Pakistan Highway Code Rule 22, no vehicle shall straddle lane markings or drive on the center line of the road. Each vehicle must remain within a single lane at all times unless changing lanes with proper signaling. Straddling lane markings is punishable by a fine of PKR 500.

Section 1.7 - Motorway Lane Restrictions
Under NHMP Motorway Rules, heavy vehicles including trucks and buses are restricted to the left lane on motorways at all times. Heavy vehicles may only use the middle lane for overtaking purposes and must return to the left lane immediately after overtaking. Violation of motorway lane restrictions for heavy vehicles carries a fine of PKR 2,000.

SECTION 2: SPEED LIMIT VIOLATIONS
====================================

Section 2.1 - General Speed Limits
Under MVO 1965, Section 40, the following speed limits apply throughout Pakistan. In residential areas and school zones: 25 km/h. In urban areas and city roads: 50 km/h. On provincial highways: 80 km/h. On national highways: 100 km/h. On motorways: 120 km/h for cars and 100 km/h for motorcycles. Heavy vehicles including trucks and buses are restricted to 80 km/h on all roads.

Section 2.2 - Speeding Penalties
NHMP Rules Chapter 6, Rule 25 defines penalties for speeding violations. Exceeding speed limit by 1-20 km/h: fine of PKR 1,000. Exceeding speed limit by 21-40 km/h: fine of PKR 2,000 and two demerit points. Exceeding speed limit by more than 40 km/h: fine of PKR 5,000, three demerit points, and possible license suspension for 60 days. Repeat offenders may face permanent license revocation.

Section 2.3 - Speed Limits in School Zones
Pakistan Highway Code, Special Provisions Section 8 mandates a speed limit of 25 km/h in school zones during school hours (7:00 AM to 4:00 PM on school days). Speed limit signs must be obeyed and violation carries a fine of PKR 2,000 and mandatory appearance before a traffic magistrate.

Section 2.4 - Speed Limits for Heavy Vehicles
Under MVO 1965, Section 41, heavy motor vehicles including trucks, buses, and trailers are subject to reduced speed limits. Maximum speed for heavy vehicles on motorways is 100 km/h. On national highways maximum speed is 80 km/h. In urban areas maximum speed is 40 km/h. Violation of heavy vehicle speed limits carries a fine of PKR 3,000 and possible vehicle detention.

Section 2.5 - Reckless Speeding
MVO 1965 Section 52 defines reckless speeding as driving at a speed which is dangerous to the public having regard to all circumstances including the nature, condition, and use of the road and the amount of traffic present. Reckless speeding is a cognizable offense punishable by imprisonment up to 6 months or a fine of PKR 10,000 or both.

Section 2.6 - Speed Enforcement on Motorways
NHMP Motorway Police Rules empower traffic officers to use speed detection equipment including radar guns and speed cameras to enforce speed limits on motorways. Evidence from speed cameras is admissible in traffic courts. Vehicle owners are liable for speeding fines captured by automated cameras even if the driver cannot be immediately identified.

Section 2.7 - Speed Limits Near Hospitals
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 31 mandates reduced speed of 30 km/h within 200 meters of hospitals, emergency services, and places of worship. Horns must not be used in these zones. Violation carries a fine of PKR 1,000.

SECTION 3: TRAFFIC SIGNAL VIOLATIONS
=======================================

Section 3.1 - Red Light Running
Under MVO 1965, Section 43, it is a serious offense to drive through a red traffic signal. Any driver who fails to stop at a red light commits an offense punishable by a fine of PKR 1,500 for the first offense, PKR 3,000 for the second offense, and license suspension for 90 days for the third offense. Red light running is considered reckless driving and endangers pedestrians and other road users.

Section 3.2 - Traffic Signal Compliance
NHMP Rules Chapter 7, Rule 28 requires all drivers to obey traffic signals at all times. Green signal means proceed with caution. Yellow signal means prepare to stop and do not enter the intersection if it is safe to stop. Red signal means stop completely before the stop line and wait until the signal turns green. Failure to comply with traffic signals is an offense under MVO 1965.

Section 3.3 - Amber Light Violations
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 35 states that when a traffic signal turns amber, drivers must stop if it is safe to do so. Accelerating through an amber light is prohibited and constitutes a traffic violation. The penalty for amber light violation is a fine of PKR 500. Drivers must not enter an intersection when the light is amber unless they are so close that stopping would be dangerous.

Section 3.4 - Traffic Signal at Intersections
Under MVO 1965, Section 44, at intersections controlled by traffic signals, all vehicles must come to a complete stop at the stop line when the signal is red. No part of the vehicle may extend beyond the stop line. Stopping beyond the stop line constitutes a traffic violation punishable by a fine of PKR 500.

Section 3.5 - Automated Signal Enforcement
NHMP Rules permit the use of automated red light cameras at intersections. Photographic evidence from red light cameras is admissible as evidence in traffic courts. Vehicle owners are liable for red light violations captured on camera. Fine notices may be issued by post to the registered owner of the vehicle.

Section 3.6 - Emergency Vehicle Signal Override
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 38 states that when emergency vehicles including police, ambulance, or fire brigade approach an intersection under emergency conditions with sirens and lights activated, all other vehicles must yield regardless of signal state. Failing to yield to an emergency vehicle at a signal carries a fine of PKR 2,000.

Section 3.7 - Signal Jumping at Night
MVO 1965 Section 45 notes that signal jumping at night is particularly dangerous as pedestrians may be crossing and visibility is reduced. Penalties for signal jumping between 10:00 PM and 6:00 AM are doubled. Night-time signal jumping carries a fine of PKR 3,000 and mandatory license suspension for 30 days.

SECTION 4: PEDESTRIAN RIGHT-OF-WAY VIOLATIONS
================================================

Section 4.1 - Pedestrian Crossing Right of Way
Under MVO 1965, Section 46, all drivers must yield to pedestrians who are at or approaching a pedestrian crossing. Failure to yield to a pedestrian at a marked crossing is a serious offense punishable by a fine of PKR 2,000 and three demerit points on the driving license. The pedestrian has absolute right of way at marked crossings.

Section 4.2 - Zebra Crossing Rules
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 40 requires vehicles to stop and give way to pedestrians at zebra crossings. A driver must not park or stop within 10 meters of a zebra crossing. Overtaking another vehicle at a zebra crossing is prohibited. Violation of zebra crossing rules carries a fine of PKR 1,000.

Section 4.3 - School Zone Pedestrian Protection
NHMP Rules Chapter 8, Rule 33 provides enhanced protection for pedestrians in school zones. Drivers must slow to 25 km/h and be prepared to stop whenever children are present near the road in school zones. Failure to yield to school children near educational institutions carries an enhanced fine of PKR 3,000 and mandatory court appearance.

Section 4.4 - Pedestrian Signal Compliance
Under MVO 1965, Section 47, where pedestrian crossing signals are installed, drivers must not enter the crossing area when the pedestrian signal shows the walking figure. Drivers must yield to all pedestrians legally crossing with the pedestrian signal. Violation carries a fine of PKR 1,500.

Section 4.5 - Footpath and Pavement Driving
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 42 strictly prohibits driving on footpaths, pavements, or pedestrian areas. Driving on a footpath to bypass traffic is a serious offense carrying a fine of PKR 3,000 and possible vehicle impoundment. Pedestrian safety zones must be respected at all times.

Section 4.6 - Pedestrian Right of Way at Roundabouts
NHMP Rules Chapter 8, Rule 35 states that pedestrians crossing at roundabout exits have right of way over vehicles entering or exiting the roundabout. Drivers must yield to pedestrians at roundabout crossings. Failure to yield at roundabout pedestrian crossings carries a fine of PKR 1,000.

Section 4.7 - Hit and Run Involving Pedestrians
MVO 1965 Section 48 makes it a criminal offense to leave the scene of an accident involving a pedestrian without stopping to render assistance and report to police. Hit and run involving a pedestrian carries imprisonment of up to 2 years, a fine of PKR 50,000, and permanent license revocation.

SECTION 5: EMERGENCY VEHICLE OBSTRUCTION
==========================================

Section 5.1 - Yielding to Emergency Vehicles
Under MVO 1965, Section 50, all drivers must immediately yield to emergency vehicles including police vehicles, ambulances, and fire brigades when they are displaying flashing lights and sounding sirens. Drivers must pull over to the left side of the road and stop until the emergency vehicle has passed. Failure to yield to an emergency vehicle is punishable by a fine of PKR 3,000.

Section 5.2 - Emergency Vehicle Corridor
NHMP Rules Chapter 9, Rule 38 requires drivers to create an emergency vehicle corridor on all multi-lane roads when emergency vehicles approach. Vehicles in left lanes must move further left and vehicles in right lanes must move further right to create a clear passage in the center. Failure to create an emergency corridor carries a fine of PKR 2,000.

Section 5.3 - Ambulance Right of Way
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 45 gives ambulances absolute right of way on all roads when responding to an emergency with lights and sirens activated. No vehicle shall obstruct, overtake, or follow closely behind an ambulance in emergency mode. Obstruction of an ambulance carries a fine of PKR 5,000 and possible imprisonment.

Section 5.4 - Fire Brigade Priority
Under MVO 1965, Section 51, fire brigade vehicles responding to emergencies have absolute priority on all roads. All traffic signals, stop signs, and road markings may be disregarded by fire brigade vehicles in emergency response. Other drivers must yield to fire brigade vehicles regardless of their own signal state. Obstruction of fire brigade vehicles carries a fine of PKR 5,000.

Section 5.5 - Police Vehicle Priority
NHMP Rules Chapter 9, Rule 40 grants police vehicles on emergency duty priority over all other road users. Drivers must not impede the progress of police vehicles displaying emergency lights and sirens. Following a police vehicle too closely during an emergency response carries a fine of PKR 1,500.

Section 5.6 - Hospital Zone Emergency Access
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 47 requires all drivers to keep hospital access roads and emergency entrances clear at all times. Parking or stopping in hospital emergency zones is strictly prohibited and carries a fine of PKR 2,000 and immediate vehicle towing at owner's expense.

Section 5.7 - Obstruction Penalties
MVO 1965 Section 53 provides that any person who willfully obstructs an emergency vehicle shall be guilty of a cognizable offense and may be arrested without warrant. The court may impose a fine of up to PKR 10,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 months for willful obstruction of emergency vehicles.

SECTION 6: RECKLESS AND NEGLIGENT DRIVING
===========================================

Section 6.1 - Definition of Reckless Driving
Under MVO 1965, Section 52, reckless driving is defined as driving a motor vehicle on a road in a manner which having regard to all the circumstances of the case is dangerous to the public. Reckless driving is a cognizable offense. First offense carries a fine of PKR 5,000 or imprisonment up to 6 months or both. Subsequent offenses carry enhanced penalties including mandatory license suspension.

Section 6.2 - Negligent Driving
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 50 defines negligent driving as driving without due care and attention or without reasonable consideration for other road users. Negligent driving is distinct from reckless driving and carries a fine of PKR 1,500 for first offense and PKR 3,000 for subsequent offenses. Three demerit points are added to the driving license.

Section 6.3 - Dangerous Overtaking
NHMP Rules Chapter 10, Rule 43 prohibits dangerous overtaking maneuvers including overtaking on blind corners, overtaking multiple vehicles simultaneously, and cutting in sharply after overtaking. Dangerous overtaking carries a fine of PKR 2,000 and two demerit points. If dangerous overtaking results in an accident, the charge may be upgraded to reckless driving.

Section 6.4 - Driving Under Influence
MVO 1965 Section 54 prohibits driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs. Any driver found to be under the influence of intoxicants while driving shall be arrested, their vehicle impounded, and they shall face imprisonment of up to 2 years and a fine of PKR 25,000 for the first offense. License is suspended immediately upon arrest.

Section 6.5 - Mobile Phone Use While Driving
Under NHMP Rules Chapter 10, Rule 45, use of a handheld mobile phone while driving is prohibited. Drivers must use hands-free devices if they need to make or receive calls. Violation carries a fine of PKR 1,000. Texting or using social media while driving is considered distracted driving and carries a fine of PKR 2,000.

Section 6.6 - Fatigue Driving
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 53 prohibits driving when fatigued or drowsy. Commercial vehicle drivers including truck and bus drivers must take mandatory rest breaks of at least 30 minutes after every 4 hours of driving. Driving beyond permitted hours carries a fine of PKR 3,000 for drivers and PKR 5,000 for vehicle owners who compel drivers to exceed driving hours.

Section 6.7 - Street Racing
MVO 1965 Section 55 strictly prohibits motor racing or speed contests on public roads. Organized or informal street racing carries a fine of PKR 10,000, vehicle impoundment for 30 days, and license suspension for 6 months. Spectators who encourage street racing may also be fined PKR 2,000.

Section 6.8 - Motorcycle Reckless Riding
NHMP Rules Chapter 10, Rule 47 specifically addresses reckless riding by motorcyclists including wheelies, lane splitting at high speed, and riding without helmet. Reckless motorcycle riding carries a fine of PKR 1,500 and possible vehicle impoundment. Riding without helmet carries an additional fine of PKR 500.

SECTION 7: VEHICLE CATEGORY RESTRICTIONS
==========================================

Section 7.1 - Heavy Vehicle Restricted Zones
Under MVO 1965, Section 58, local authorities may designate restricted zones where heavy vehicles including trucks, trailers, and buses above a specified weight are prohibited. Heavy vehicles found in restricted zones are subject to a fine of PKR 5,000 and may be directed to return via an approved route. Repeated violations result in commercial vehicle permit suspension.

Section 7.2 - Time-Based Heavy Vehicle Restrictions
NHMP Rules Chapter 11, Rule 50 permits time-based restrictions on heavy vehicles in urban areas. In many Pakistani cities, heavy vehicles are prohibited from entering city centers between 7:00 AM and 10:00 PM. Violation of time-based restrictions carries a fine of PKR 3,000. Heavy vehicle operators must plan routes to avoid restricted zones during prohibited hours.

Section 7.3 - Weight Limit Violations
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 57 sets maximum weight limits for vehicles on different road categories. Overloaded vehicles cause significant road damage and are a safety hazard. Vehicles exceeding weight limits are subject to fines based on the degree of overloading. Fine is PKR 1,000 per ton of excess weight. Vehicles may be detained until excess load is removed.

Section 7.4 - Bus Route Restrictions
Under MVO 1965, Section 59, public buses must operate only on designated routes approved by the relevant transport authority. Operating a bus on a non-designated route is an offense carrying a fine of PKR 2,000 and possible suspension of the route permit. Passengers safety must not be compromised by operating unauthorized routes.

Section 7.5 - Truck Parking Restrictions
NHMP Rules Chapter 11, Rule 52 restricts truck parking in residential areas, near schools, hospitals, and markets. Trucks may only park in designated truck parking areas. Illegal truck parking carries a fine of PKR 1,500 and vehicle may be towed to designated parking at owner's expense.

Section 7.6 - Hazardous Materials Transport
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 59 requires vehicles carrying hazardous materials to display appropriate warning signs, carry relevant documentation, and follow designated routes for hazardous materials transport. Violation of hazardous materials transport regulations carries a fine of PKR 10,000 and possible criminal charges.

Section 7.7 - Motorcycle Restrictions
Under MVO 1965, Section 60, motorcycles are prohibited on motorways in Pakistan. Motorcycles may not use motorway entry or exit ramps. Motorcycle riders found on motorways are subject to a fine of PKR 1,000 and their vehicle will be removed from the motorway. Motorcycles are also restricted from some urban expressways as designated by local authorities.

Section 7.8 - Public Transport Overloading
NHMP Rules Chapter 11, Rule 54 prohibits public transport vehicles from carrying more passengers than their licensed seating capacity. Overloaded buses and minibuses are subject to a fine of PKR 2,000 and must off-load excess passengers before proceeding. The driver and vehicle owner are both liable for overloading violations.

Section 7.9 - Agricultural Vehicle Restrictions
Pakistan Highway Code Rule 61 restricts agricultural vehicles including tractors and combine harvesters from using motorways and high-speed highways. Agricultural vehicles may only use local roads and provincial highways with a maximum speed of 40 km/h. Violation carries a fine of PKR 1,000.

Section 7.10 - Three-Wheeler Restrictions
Under MVO 1965, Section 61, three-wheeled vehicles including rickshaws are prohibited from using motorways, national highways, and some urban expressways. Three-wheelers found on restricted roads are subject to a fine of PKR 1,000 and immediate removal from the restricted road.
